# best crypto exchange: how to compare fees, tiers and listings before you sign up

Search that phrase and you get a stack of rankings, each with a different winner, most of them sponsored somewhere in the funnel. The other problem is subtler: whoever "wins" a ranking usually won because they fit the reviewer's trading habits, not yours. A day trader paying 0.02% maker fees, someone buying $200 of bitcoin every payday, and a person hunting a token that listed three days ago need three different platforms.

So the useful version of this question isn't "which one is best." It's "which five things do I check, in what order, and what do the numbers actually look like right now." Below is that checklist, worked through with current published numbers from Gate (gate.com) so the comparison isn't abstract.

## Five questions that decide it

1. **What will you actually do with the account?** Monthly buys and holds, long-tail altcoin trades, futures, or sitting on a balance and earning yield.
2. **What's the entry fee you'll pay on day one?** Not the smallest number in some tier table you'll never reach — the rate on your first trade, before any volume discount.
3. **Does it list what you want to buy?** A cheap exchange with 70 coins is useless if your token isn't one of them.
4. **Can you verify the money is there?** Proof of reserves, which assets it covers, and who audited it.
5. **Can you even open an account?** This one kills more shortlists than fees ever do.

Order matters. A platform that blocks your country doesn't need a fee analysis.

## The fee math, layer by layer

Published spot fees are only the first line of the bill. Four layers stack up:

- **Maker vs taker.** Limit orders that rest in the book pay maker fees; orders that fill immediately pay taker fees. Some exchanges charge the same for both, which quietly penalises anyone who uses limit orders.
- **Futures rates.** On Gate, USDT-margined perpetuals start at **0.02% maker / 0.05% taker** at the base tier. Points can be used to offset taker fees, with the combined rate going as low as 0.0225% according to Gate's futures fee documentation. Options are cheaper still: **0.02% maker / 0.028% taker** at the base tier.
- **Token discounts.** Holding and using the platform token lowers the rate further. On Gate's fee page, VIP 0 with GT deduction enabled is **0.09% / 0.09%** instead of **0.10% / 0.10%**.
- **Everything the headline hides.** Instant-buy spreads (often 0.4%–1.5% on consumer screens), withdrawal fees per network, card deposit surcharges.

That last layer is where beginners lose the most. Buying through an instant-buy button at a 1% spread on a $5,000 purchase costs $50 — more than a year of order-book fees for a casual trader.

## What this table tells you about your own trading

If you trade spot only, a monthly volume under 60,000 USD keeps you at VIP 0, which means the only lever you have is GT deduction: 0.10% to 0.09%. That's a 10% cut on every trade for holding a token you might already want.

Futures traders hit thresholds far faster because volume is weighted at 40% of notional — an account doing $2.5M in monthly perpetual volume carries the same weight as $1M in spot. That's the intended path for active derivative traders.

Asset-heavy, low-frequency accounts get a different route entirely: $40,000 in qualifying account value reaches VIP 5 without trading a thing. Which route is cheapest depends on whether you'd rather deploy capital or generate volume.

## New-user rewards: the headline number versus the payout

Gate's Rewards Hub advertises a task-based welcome package and, above it, advanced deposit-and-trade challenges. The headline figures you'll see depend on your region and the campaign running at the time — different regional versions of the same page currently show figures of 10,000+ USDT and 15,000 USDT, and a welcome package total that varies between regional versions. The structure beneath it is consistent:

- Register and log in
- Complete identity verification (including face verification)
- Make a first deposit (the threshold shown varies between regional versions)
- Place a first spot or futures trade above a small minimum
- Download the app

Then the advanced tasks stack on top: net deposit thresholds combined with cumulative trading volume, unlocked in stages up to the headline figure.

Before you count any of it as money in your pocket, three things worth knowing. Rewards are typically issued as **position vouchers or fee credits rather than withdrawable cash** — useful for reducing trading costs, not for cashing out. They usually come with a lock period (roughly 14 days in several Gate programmes) before they can be used freely. And the advanced tiers require serious volume, so the 10,000+ USDT figure is a ladder ceiling, not a signup gift.

If your plan is to accumulate a position slowly, the reward ladder is mostly irrelevant to you — the fee structure is what determines your real cost. If you're an active trader, the trading-volume rungs are worth working through deliberately.

## Quick answers

**Is 0.1% a good spot trading fee?** It's at the competitive end for a base tier — the same as Binance's regular-user rate, and well below Kraken Pro and Gemini ActiveTrader at entry. Below it you're looking at MEXC-style zero-maker schedules and higher-tier programmes.

**Do I need GT to get lower fees?** No — the tier path via 30-day volume works without holding anything. GT deduction is a separate discount that stacks on top of whatever tier you're in.

**How long until a tier upgrade shows up?** Gate evaluates accounts on a rolling basis, with tier updates following qualification; volume-based upgrades carry roughly 60 days of protection before step-downs begin.

**Are the reward vouchers real money?** They're usable credits, not cash. Treat them as a trading-cost discount with a lock period, not as a deposit bonus you can withdraw.

**What if I'm in the US, UK or Canada?** The global platform isn't the route. Look at venues licensed for your jurisdiction instead — the trade-off will be a smaller asset list and higher entry fees.
